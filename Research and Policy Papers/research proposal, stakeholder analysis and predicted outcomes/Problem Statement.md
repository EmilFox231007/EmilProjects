
The adoption of generative AI in small to medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) is affected by a lack of understanding about the current level of knowledge and willingness of employees to adopt this technology in their roles, as well as their preferred methods for acquiring the necessary skills. This knowledge gap affects not only the employees themselves, who are concerned about job security and the relevance of their skills, but also SME owners and managers, who are responsible for making strategic decisions about AI adoption and are concerned about return on investment, employee retention, and competitive advantage.

Furthermore, the successful integration of generative AI in SMEs also depends on the support of industry associations and chambers of commerce, government and policymakers, educational institutions and training providers, AI and technology developers, and customers and clients of SMEs. These stakeholders have varying levels of power, attention, and interest in AI adoption, but all play a critical role in shaping the ecosystem that enables or hampers the adoption of generative AI in SMEs.

By investigating the current level of knowledge, willingness, and preferred learning methods among employees in SMEs, this research aims to provide valuable insights that can inform strategies for successful adoption and integration of generative AI in these organizations, ultimately benefiting all stakeholders involved.
