#### 1. **SME Owners and Managers**

- **Power:** High
- **Attention:** High
- **Placement:** **Key Players**
- **Why:** SME owners and managers are the primary decision-makers regarding AI adoption in their companies. They have significant influence over how AI is integrated and how employees are trained. They are highly interested in the outcomes because these will directly impact their business’s performance and competitive edge.

#### 2. **SME Employees**

- **Power:** Low
- **Attention:** High
- **Placement:** **Keep Informed**
- **Why:** SME employees are directly affected by AI adoption, particularly concerning job security and the need for reskilling. While they may not have as much decision-making power as owners or managers, their collective response and willingness to engage with AI are critical to successful implementation. Their high level of interest requires that they be kept informed and engaged throughout the process.

#### 3. **Government and Policymakers**

- **Power:** High
- **Attention:** Low
- **Placement:** **Manage Closely**
- **Why:** Governments and policymakers have significant power to influence the regulatory environment, funding, and support for AI adoption in SMEs. Their attention level might vary depending on how AI impacts economic growth, job creation, and social stability. Given their high power, they should be closely managed to ensure that policies align with the needs of SMEs and their employees.

#### 4. **Educational Institutions and Training Providers**

- **Power:** Low
- **Attention:** Low
- **Placement:** **Keep Satisfied**
- **Why lol:** Educational institutions and training providers have the power to shape the workforce’s readiness for AI through the courses and training programs they offer. They have a moderate level of attention, as they are interested in aligning their offerings with market needs but are not the primary drivers of AI adoption. They should be kept satisfied by ensuring their programs are aligned with industry requirements.
